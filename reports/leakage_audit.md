# Leakage audit: duplicate notes and specialty classification

Generated by `src/leakage_audit.py`.

Same model (TF-IDF + logistic regression), same 3,584 rows (2,165 unique notes, 10 most common specialties), 5 random seeds (mean ± standard deviation). Only the train/test split changes.

| Metric | Random split | Grouped split (by note) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Test notes also seen in training | 63% ± 2% | 0% ± 0% |
| Accuracy | 26% ± 1% | 55% ± 1% |
| Macro F1 | 0.18 ± 0.01 | 0.45 ± 0.02 |
| Any-label accuracy* | 96% ± 0% | 93% ± 2% |
| Accuracy on test notes seen in training | 0% ± 0% | n/a |
| Accuracy on test notes not seen in training | 71% ± 2% | 55% ± 1% |
| Seen notes: model repeats a label from the training copy | 100% ± 0% | n/a |

\* Counted as correct if the predicted specialty is any of the specialties the note is filed under.

## What this shows

- **With a random split, 63% of test notes are copies of training notes.**
- **The model memorizes rather than generalizes on those copies.** It returns a label it saw on the training copy 100% of the time. Because the test copy is filed under a different specialty, plain accuracy on these notes is close to zero.
- **So a random split makes scores misleading in both directions:** plain accuracy is pushed down (memorized labels are marked wrong), while any-label accuracy is pushed up (memorized labels count as right).
- **The sound setup** keeps all copies of a note in the same split and evaluates against all of a note's labels. That is the grouped column.
- **The specialty label itself is ambiguous:** one note can be correctly filed under several specialties, so no model can reach 100% plain accuracy on this data.
